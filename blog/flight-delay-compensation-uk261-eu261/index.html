Flight delay compensation: what you can claim under UK and EU rules

A long delay can wreck a holiday, but many passengers don't know that fixed compensation may be owed on top of food and a hotel. Here is what the UK Civil Aviation Authority and the EU's Your Europe service say, as of November 2026, and how to start a claim.

How much is flight delay compensation?

The amounts are fixed and per person, and don't depend on what you paid for the ticket. The UK CAA gives these figures for UK rules: £220 for flights under 1,500 km, £350 for 1,500 to 3,500 km, and for flights over 3,500 km £260 if you arrive 3 to 4 hours late or £520 if you arrive more than 4 hours late.

The EU's Your Europe service lists €250 (1,500 km or less), €400 (over 1,500 km within the EU, and other flights of 1,500 to 3,500 km) and €600 (over 3,500 km). Which regime applies depends on the flight, so check the CAA and Your Europe pages below for your route and airline.

When do you qualify for compensation?

Compensation is about arrival time: you generally need to reach your final destination around three hours or more late. Airlines don't have to pay if the delay was caused by "extraordinary circumstances". Your Europe gives examples such as air traffic management decisions, political instability, adverse weather and security risks, and says most technical problems and strikes by the airline's own staff don't count. The airline has to show the link to the event and that it couldn't have been avoided.

Still, even where compensation isn't due, your right to care and assistance (next section) can apply.

What must the airline give you while you wait?

The CAA says airlines should provide food and drink appropriate to the length of the delay (usually as a voucher), two phone calls or emails, and, if you're delayed overnight, accommodation and transport to and from it. These duties start once your departure is delayed by at least 2 hours (flights under 1,500 km), 3 hours (1,500 to 3,500 km) or 4 hours (over 3,500 km).

If the delay passes five hours, the CAA says you can decline to travel and ask for a refund of the unused flights, though that only makes sense if you no longer want the trip. Keep itemised receipts for anything you pay for yourself, because the airline will want them.

How to claim flight delay compensation

It isn't paid automatically. Both the CAA and Your Europe say to claim from the airline first, using its complaint form, and to keep your booking confirmation, boarding pass and receipts. Expenses and compensation may need separate claims.

  • UK flights: if the airline hasn't answered within eight weeks, or you're unhappy with its final response, the CAA says to go to the airline's alternative dispute resolution (ADR) provider if it has one, or otherwise to the CAA's Passenger Advice and Complaints Team.
  • EU: Your Europe says that after two months without a reply, or an unsatisfactory one, you can complain to the national enforcement body in the country where the incident happened, or use ADR or small claims procedures.

The CAA page doesn't state a claim deadline, and court time limits follow national rules, so don't wait: claim soon after you travel.

Cutting the risk when you book

You can't promise yourself an on-time flight, but you can plan for a bad day. Leave a generous gap on any connection, and be careful with separate tickets, where a delay on the first flight may not be covered by the second airline. Frequently asked questions

How long does a flight have to be delayed to claim compensation?
Under both the UK and EU rules described by the CAA and Your Europe, the usual threshold is arriving at your final destination three hours or more late, unless the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances.
Do I get compensation if bad weather delays my flight?
Your Europe lists adverse weather as an example of extraordinary circumstances, so compensation may not be due. You can still be entitled to food, drink and accommodation while you wait.
Is flight delay compensation paid automatically?
No. The CAA says you must claim from the airline first. If it refuses or doesn't reply within eight weeks, you can escalate to an ADR provider or the CAA's Passenger Advice and Complaints Team.
