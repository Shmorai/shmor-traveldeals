Christmas market city breaks: Europe’s best festive getaways

Mulled wine, twinkling stalls and historic squares. These are the European Christmas markets travellers search for most, with tips for planning your trip.

By the TravelDeals editorial team · 13 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Fas Khan on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Germany: where the tradition started
  2. Austria, Czechia and Hungary
  3. France, Poland and Scandinavia
  4. When the markets open
  5. Tips for a Christmas market break

Germany: where the tradition started

  • Nuremberg: the Christkindlesmarkt is one of Germany’s most famous markets, known for gingerbread (Lebkuchen) and handmade decorations.
  • Cologne: several markets across the city, including one beside the cathedral. Good for a long weekend.
  • Many other German cities, including Dresden, Munich and Berlin, have their own historic markets.

Austria, Czechia and Hungary

  • Vienna: markets in front of the City Hall, at Schönbrunn Palace and in smaller squares across the city, with plenty of coffee houses for warming up.
  • Prague: the Old Town Square market, with its Christmas tree in front of the Gothic Church of Our Lady before Týn, is a favourite for festive photos.
  • Budapest: the market on Vörösmarty Square and the one by St Stephen’s Basilica combine Hungarian food with festive lights.

France, Poland and Scandinavia

  • Strasbourg: calls itself the “Capital of Christmas” and has one of the oldest Christmas markets in France.
  • Kraków: the market on the Main Market Square (Rynek Główny) offers great value for a festive city break.
  • Copenhagen: Tivoli Gardens’ Christmas season and the city’s hygge atmosphere make it a cosy alternative.

When the markets open

Most of Europe’s Christmas markets run from late November until just before Christmas. A few stay open after 25 December or into early January. Dates change every year, so check the city’s official tourism website before you book.

Tips for a Christmas market break

  • Go midweek for smaller crowds and often lower hotel prices.
  • Book early. December weekends are the busiest time in the most popular cities.
  • Stay central so you can walk between markets in the evening.
  • Pack for the cold, and bring a card and some cash for stalls.

Frequently asked questions

When do European Christmas markets open?
Most open in late November and close around 23 or 24 December. Some continue after Christmas. Check the official dates for your city each year.
Which Christmas market is best for a weekend break?
Compact cities with several markets close together work best, such as Vienna, Prague, Cologne, Strasbourg and Kraków.
