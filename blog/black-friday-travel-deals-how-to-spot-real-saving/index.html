Black Friday travel deals: how to tell a real saving from a pushy sales pitch

Black Friday falls on 27 November in 2026, and Cyber Monday follows on 30 November. Flights, hotels and package holidays all get "deal" labels this week. Here is how to check whether the price you see is the price you will pay, and how to compare offers without rushing.

Why Black Friday travel deals need a second look

Big sale weeks create pressure to book fast. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) says people need to know that "the price they see is the price they'll pay" and that sales should be genuine. Since the CMA gained direct enforcement powers in April 2025 it has been looking hard at online pricing, and in November 2025 it opened its first investigations, including into whether time-limited sales really ended when advertised. As of November 2025 it had reached no conclusions in those cases, so treat that as a sign of where regulators are looking, not a verdict on any travel brand.

For you, the practical point is simple: a bigger discount label is not proof of a better deal. Check the price yourself.

Check the total price, not the headline price

"Drip pricing" means extra charges appear one step at a time during checkout. The CMA's guidance says mandatory charges should be included in the price you are first shown. Optional extras, such as a hold bag or seat choice on a flight, are different, so the fair test is whether you could avoid the charge.

  • Flights: price the whole trip: the fare, the bag you actually need, and a seat if you are travelling as a family. Compare that total across airlines and agents on our cheap flights page.
  • Package holidays: check whether transfers, baggage and meals are included, and what the deposit and final payment dates are.
  • Hotels: look for resort or city taxes that are paid locally, and whether breakfast is part of the rate.

Where a price is quoted in dollars or euros, convert it, and note any card fees.

Fake urgency: countdown timers and "only 2 left"

The CMA has said misleading countdown timers are banned under the new regime. A timer that resets when you refresh the page, or a sale that quietly continues after it "ends", should make you more cautious, not less. Prices on flights and hotels do genuinely change as seats and rooms sell, so the reliable approach is to compare, not to guess whether a timer is real.

A useful habit: open the same trip in two or three places, note the total price, and wait an hour. If the price is the same, you lose nothing by deciding calmly.

Compare like with like

A cheap-looking holiday often differs from a dearer one in ways the headline hides. Before comparing, line up the same dates, board basis (room only, bed and breakfast, all-inclusive), airport, baggage and transfers. Our guides to package holidays and last-minute holidays explain what to compare. If you are choosing between brands, you can look at flights through a UK travel agent's flight search or run the same search on several sites to see how totals differ.

Know what protection you have before you pay

A cut price is no use if the booking is not protected. Package holidays sold in the UK may carry ATOL or other protection, and paying by credit card can add rights on larger purchases. We explain how to check this in our guide to ATOL, ABTA and credit cards. Also read the change and cancellation terms: some sale fares are non-refundable, which matters if your plans could shift.

A five-step checklist before you click Book

  1. Write down the total price for the exact dates, bags and passengers.
  2. Check mandatory fees and taxes are in that total.
  3. Compare at least one other site or provider.
  4. Read the cancellation and change terms and the payment schedule.
  5. Confirm the protection that applies (ATOL, card payment) and keep your confirmation.

If a deal passes all five, it is likely worth taking, whatever day of the week it is.

Frequently asked questions

When is Black Friday in 2026?
Black Friday is on 27 November 2026, with Cyber Monday on 30 November. Many travel firms start their sales earlier in the week and some run them through December.
Are Black Friday travel deals really cheaper?
Sometimes, but not always. Price the whole trip including bags, transfers and fees, then compare it with the same trip on other sites. A big discount percentage does not show the final price is low.
What is drip pricing?
It is when extra charges are added gradually as you book. The CMA's guidance says mandatory charges should be included in the headline price rather than appearing late in checkout.
Is a countdown timer on a travel site reliable?
Not necessarily. The CMA says misleading countdown timers are banned, but you cannot tell from the page whether one is accurate, so compare prices elsewhere before deciding.
