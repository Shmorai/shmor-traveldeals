UK airport liquids rule: the 100ml limit, 2-litre airports and what to check

The 100ml liquids rule is changing airport by airport, which makes packing for a holiday confusing, especially on trips with connecting flights. Here is what GOV.UK says, and a simple way to avoid a bin-side repack.

By the TravelDeals editorial team · 18 November 2026 · 2 min read

What is the hand luggage liquids rule in the UK?

GOV.UK says that at most airports you cannot take liquids in containers larger than 100ml through security, and the limit applies even to partly filled containers. It also says that some airports may allow containers holding up to 2 litres, and tells passengers to check with the airport before they travel. So the answer depends on where you fly from, and (as GOV.UK points out) on any connecting airports too.

Which UK airports allow 2 litres?

Lists of airports have been changing and are reported differently across the media, so we don't give one here. The reliable check is the departure airport's own website, shortly before you travel. GOV.UK doesn't list airports on its liquids page; it simply says rules vary. If you are flying out of one airport and back into another, check both.

Do I still need a clear plastic bag?

According to GOV.UK, some airports require liquids to go in a separate bag and some do not. Where it is required, it should be a single, transparent, resealable plastic bag holding no more than a litre (about 20cm x 20cm), sealed rather than tied, one per person, and shown at the security point. Again, check your airport.

What can I always take through security?

GOV.UK lists exemptions allowing up to 2 litres at all UK airports: liquids for essential medical purposes, food for special dietary requirements, and baby food or baby milk. Duty-free and in-flight purchases can go through if sealed in a security bag at purchase with the receipt inside and visible, and not opened until you reach your final destination. Frozen liquids are not allowed in hand luggage. Security staff can open items for screening.

Packing tips for holidays with connecting flights

If your trip has more than one leg, the strictest airport on your route sets the rule for liquids you carry through it, so packing to 100ml is the safe default. Where you can, put liquids in your checked bag. If you are flying on a budget airline with a small cabin allowance, check the airline's rules in our cheap flights guide, and for family trips with baby food or milk see our family holidays page.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 100ml liquids rule still in force in the UK?
At most airports, yes. GOV.UK says that at most airports you cannot take liquids in containers larger than 100ml through security, though some airports may allow containers of up to 2 litres. Check your airport before you travel.
Can I take baby milk through UK airport security?
Yes. GOV.UK lists baby food or baby milk among the exemptions that allow up to 2 litres at all UK airports, along with essential medicines and special dietary food.
Can I take duty-free liquids in hand luggage?
Yes, if they are sealed in a security bag when you buy them, with the receipt sealed inside and visible, and you don't open the bag before your final destination.
