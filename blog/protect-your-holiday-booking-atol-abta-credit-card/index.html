How to protect your holiday booking: ATOL, ABTA and paying by credit card

A great deal is only a great deal if you get the holiday you paid for. Before you book, it takes a few minutes to check what happens if an airline or travel company collapses, or if something goes wrong.

Why holiday booking protection matters

Most holidays go smoothly. The rare ones that don’t can be costly: an airline or tour operator stops trading, a hotel isn’t what was advertised, or you can’t get home. Different bookings carry different protection, and the cheapest-looking option isn’t always the one that protects you best. The points below follow UK government guidance (as of November 2026); if you live elsewhere, your own country’s rules and regulators apply.

ATOL protection for flights and package holidays

The UK’s Air Travel Organisers’ Licence (ATOL) scheme protects travellers who book a holiday that includes a flight. According to GOV.UK, if an ATOL-licensed company collapses, the scheme is there to make sure your money is protected and you can get home. ATOL is run by the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA).

Two limits are worth knowing:

  • Most bookings made directly with an airline are not covered by ATOL. If you buy a flight-only ticket straight from the airline, other protections apply instead (see the credit card and insurance sections below).
  • Package sales made in the UK by travel businesses established in the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway are not covered by ATOL. Those bookings must be protected under the system in the company’s home country.

You can check whether a business holds an ATOL using the CAA’s Check an ATOL search before you pay. If you’re comparing package holidays, it’s a quick step that’s easy to skip.

What ABTA membership means

The Association of British Travel Agents (ABTA) is a trade body. GOV.UK explains that holidays booked with ABTA members give you financial and legal protection, and that the travel company is responsible for making sure you get the holiday you paid for. If something isn’t provided or isn’t as expected, the company or its suppliers are responsible for resolving it, by offering an alternative or a full or partial refund. In some cases you may be able to claim compensation.

ABTA and ATOL are different things. ATOL is about flights and flight-inclusive holidays; ABTA membership is about the company you book with. A single booking can have both.

Pay for your holiday by credit card where you can

GOV.UK advises booking travel and holidays with a credit card where possible, because it should give you some protection if your travel firm goes bust. You may be able to get a refund for credit card payments between £100 and £30,000. This is one reason a flight-only booking made directly with an airline is often best paid for with a credit card.

A few practical notes:

  • The details depend on your card provider and how the booking was paid for, so ask them before relying on it.
  • Keep your booking confirmation and receipts.

What travel insurance does and doesn’t cover

GOV.UK says you should buy travel insurance as soon as possible after booking, and read the small print and exclusions. Check whether your policy provides cover if an airline or travel agent goes out of business. GOV.UK notes this is typically not covered by standard policies, so you may want to choose an ATOL-protected holiday or a policy that includes airline or supplier failure cover.

Entry rules are another thing that can change your plans: see our guide to EU entry rules for rules that can change your plans, and check your destination’s travel advice before you go.

A quick checklist before you pay

  1. Is it a package with a flight? Look for an ATOL number and check it with the CAA.
  2. Is the company an ABTA member? Check on ABTA’s own site.
  3. Can you pay by credit card?
  4. Does your travel insurance include airline or supplier failure?
  5. Do you have the booking terms, including cancellation and change rules, saved?

Once you’re happy with the protection, you can compare flights, all-inclusive holidays and last-minute holidays with more confidence.

Frequently asked questions

Is a flight-only ticket covered by ATOL?
Usually not. GOV.UK says the majority of bookings made directly with airlines are not covered by ATOL. ATOL is aimed at holidays that include a flight, so consider paying by credit card and having insurance that covers airline failure.
Does paying by credit card protect my holiday?
It can. GOV.UK says booking with a credit card should give you some protection if a travel firm goes bust, and you may be able to claim back payments between £100 and £30,000. Check the details with your card provider.
Does standard travel insurance cover airline collapse?
Typically not, according to GOV.UK. Look for a policy that specifically includes airline or supplier failure cover, or choose an ATOL-protected holiday.
